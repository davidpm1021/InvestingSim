A brokerage account is where you buy and hold investments like stocks, funds, and bonds. Just like online banking, most brokerages let you manage everything from a computer or phone: moving money in, placing trades, and tracking how your investments perform. In this activity you will use a *simulation* to practice opening and using a brokerage account, so you feel prepared to use a REAL one someday.

***Note: This is a simulation and you should NOT use any of your actual bank or brokerage account information at any point. Any names, account numbers, and balances you see pre-entered are fictional.***

> **Teacher Tips:**
> - There is no teacher dashboard, so you cannot check students' work in progress. To start a student over, use the **Reset** button in the top bar, which clears their transactions and holdings.
> - **Time moves one way.** Students advance with the **"Jump to [next quarter]"** button and confirm a warning each time. They **cannot go back** to a previous quarter. If a student needs to redo a quarter, a teacher can reach the **Admin Panel** (Account menu, top right) to override the quarter.
> - The sim is **evergreen**: it shows quarters and months but never a year. Your students' specific dollar results will differ depending on what they buy, so most answers below are open-ended.
> - **Dividends, bond income, and interest post automatically.** Students do not set these up. Interest posts monthly; dividends and bond income post at the end of each quarter into the brokerage cash.

Congrats on opening your very first brokerage account at **Summit Invest**! You already have a savings account at **Evergreen Bank** with **$5,000** in it, ready to put to work.

- [ ] Open the **Brokerage Simulator** and click the **Web Browser** icon on the desktop. It opens to **Summit Invest**, your new brokerage.

## Part I: Set Up and Connect Your Bank

Before you can invest, your brokerage needs a way to get money in and out. Let's get connected and look around.

- [ ] On the Summit Invest **Overview** tab, click **"Connect your bank."** Review the pre-filled details, then click **"Connect to your Bank"** and watch it link.

**1.** When you first open Summit Invest, it asks you to connect a bank account before you can do anything else.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** Why do you think a brokerage makes you connect a bank first?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A brokerage holds your investments, but the cash to buy them has to come from somewhere. Connecting your bank lets you move money into the brokerage to invest, and move it back out when you want it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** Whose account are you connecting, and to which bank?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Sample Student's account at Evergreen Bank (account ending 1234).

**2.** You now have two accounts: a **Savings** account at Evergreen Bank and a **Cash Settlement Account** at Summit Invest.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** What is the balance of each account right now?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Evergreen Bank Savings: **$5,000.00**. Summit Invest Cash Settlement Account: **$0.00**.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** Each account lists an APY. What are they, and which pays more?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Savings is **1.50% APY**; the Cash Settlement Account is **0.25% APY**. The bank savings pays more.

**3.** The Cash Settlement Account pays less interest than your savings. Knowing that, why would you ever move money into the brokerage at all?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] The settlement account is just a holding spot for cash that is waiting to be invested or withdrawn. You don't move money there to earn interest. You move it there so you can buy investments, which have the potential to grow faster than cash sitting in either account.

## Part II: Move Money In (Make a Transfer)

You can't invest cash that's still sitting at the bank. Time to fund your brokerage.

- [ ] On the **Overview** tab, click **"Add Funds."** Transfer an amount of your choice from Evergreen Bank into your Summit Invest Cash Settlement Account. (Leaving some money in savings is fine.)

**4.** How much did you transfer, and what are your two balances now?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers will vary] Example: I transferred $4,000, so Evergreen Bank Savings shows $1,000.00 and my Summit Invest Cash Settlement Account shows $4,000.00. Notice the total across both accounts did not change. You only moved the money.

**5.** The transfer screen says the transfer is "instant in this simulation," but in real life it "can take a few days to process." Why is that good to know before you invest?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] If a real transfer takes a few days to clear, you can't buy investments with that money the instant you click. You would need to move cash in ahead of time and not count on it being available immediately.

**6.** Open the **Evergreen Bank** tab and look at its Activity. What entry shows the money leaving, and did the total amount of money you have change?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The Activity shows a **"Transfer to brokerage"** entry that lowers your savings balance. The total money you have did not change. A transfer just moves it from one account to the other.

> **Teacher Tip:** The transfer is reversible. The same screen offers **"Withdraw Funds"** to move money from the brokerage back to the bank. You may want students to try both directions so they see the brokerage and bank as two separate places their money can live.

## Part III: Build Your Portfolio (Buy)

Now the fun part. The **Daily Movers** section on the Overview shows the investments you can buy.

- [ ] Click a few **Daily Movers** to read their details. Then click **"Buy"** and notice the investments are grouped into **Stocks, Funds, and Bonds**. Buy **at least three different investments of different types**, using either a dollar amount or a number of shares.

**7.** There are seven investments to choose from. What are the different *types* available?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Three individual **stocks** (Harvest Foods Co., Granite Power & Light, Sterling Health), one **mutual fund** (Total Stock Market Index Fund), one **ETF** (Total Stock Market ETF), one **target-date fund** (Target Date 2070 Fund), and one **bond fund** (Total Bond Market Fund).

**8.** Two of the investments track the same thing, the total U.S. stock market: one is a mutual fund, the other is an ETF. According to their descriptions, what is the difference in how they trade?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A **mutual fund** prices once per day, after the market closes. An **ETF** trades throughout the day at the market price, like a stock.

**9.** When you set up a buy, a note appears about fees and settlement.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** What does **"commission-free"** mean?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;There is no fee to buy or sell.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** The note also mentions settlement. In real life, when does cash from a sale typically become available?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The next business day (T+1).

**10.** After buying, look at the **Gain/Loss** on one of your holdings (Holdings tab, or the Holdings table on Overview). Right after you buy, what is the gain/loss, and why?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It is about **0%** (basically $0). You just bought at the current market price, so what you paid and what it is worth are the same. A gain or loss only shows up once the price moves.

**11.** You bought more than one type of investment. Why is spreading your money across different investments (diversifying) generally smarter than putting it all in one?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] If all your money is in one investment and it drops, your whole portfolio drops with it. Spreading across different companies and asset types means a bad result in one is cushioned by the others.

## Part IV: Let Time Pass (Advance Quarters and Read Your Statement)

One quarter of investing is done. Let's move time forward and see what happened.

- [ ] In the top bar, click **"Jump to Quarter 2."** Read the confirmation, then click **Continue**.

**12.** A confirmation box appears before you advance. What does it warn you about, and why does it matter?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It warns you to finalize your trades first, because once you move to the next quarter you **cannot go back**. It matters because time only moves forward, so you can't undo or redo a quarter's decisions.

**13.** After advancing, you get a notification.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** What does it say?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**"Your Quarter 1 statement is ready."**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** Open the **Notifications** bell (top right). What kinds of things does this brokerage notify you about, and what does it NOT do?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answer] It sends factual account alerts only: statements being ready and trade/order confirmations. It does **not** give advice or tell you what to buy or sell, just like a real brokerage.

- [ ] Open the Summit Invest **Activity** tab, then open your **Evergreen Bank** Activity.

**14.** Look at both accounts' Activity. You will see entries you did not create yourself.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** In your Evergreen Bank savings, what new entry appears at the end of the month?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;An **"Interest payment"** entry. Your savings earns interest, paid monthly.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** In your Summit Invest account, if you held investments through the end of the quarter, what cash entries show up?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**Dividends** from the stocks and stock funds, and **bond fund income** if you own the bond fund. Both post as cash into your Cash Settlement Account at the end of the quarter.

**15.** Go to the **Statements** tab and open your **Quarter 1 statement** (use "How to read this statement" if you need it).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** The statement separates **"Cash Available"** from **"Investments."** What is the difference?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Cash Available is your uninvested settlement cash. Investments is the value of what you own (your stocks, funds, and bonds).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** In **"Your Investments,"** compare **"What You Paid"** to the **"Current Value"** for one holding. Is the gain or loss shown there money you actually have yet? Why or why not?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] No. It is an **unrealized** gain or loss. It only becomes real money once you sell. Until then, the value can still go up or down.

- [ ] **Sell** part or all of one holding. Then look at the **Activity** tab.

**16.** When you sell, the Activity tags the sale with a realized gain or loss marked **"ST"** or **"LT."**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**a.** What do ST and LT stand for?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Short-term and long-term.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**b.** In this simulation almost every sale is short-term. Why?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answer] Short-term means you held the investment one year or less. Because the whole simulation covers about a year, you will rarely hold anything longer than that, so gains are almost always short-term.

- [ ] Keep advancing one quarter at a time through **Quarter 4**, buying or selling as you like, and open each new statement when it is ready.

**17.** As you move through the quarters, watch the **"Compare Investments"** chart on the Overview and the **"Portfolio Value Over Time"** chart on Holdings. What is one thing you notice about how your investments moved?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] Example: the stocks and stock funds rose and fell more than the bond fund, which stayed fairly flat.

> **Teacher Tip:** Income only posts for investments held **through the end of a quarter**. A student who buys and then sells everything inside the same quarter will not see dividends. Statements appear only **after** a quarter ends, so the first statement shows up once a student advances out of Quarter 1.

## Part V: Year-End Review and Reflect

- [ ] From Quarter 4, advance one last time to the **Year-End Review**.

**18.** Open the Year-End Review. Compare your total account value now to the amount you actually added (transferred in). Did your account grow, and is all of that growth "real"?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] Example: my account is worth more than I put in. The gain on investments I still hold is **unrealized**; the dividends, interest, and any sales I made are **realized** cash. Whether it grew at all depends on how the investments I chose performed.

**19.** Think about the two cash accounts (Savings at 1.50%, Settlement at 0.25%) versus the investments you bought. What is the trade-off between keeping money in cash and investing it?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;[Answers may vary] Cash is steady and safe but grows slowly. Investments can grow faster but can also fall in value. The right mix depends on when you need the money and how much ups and downs you can handle.
